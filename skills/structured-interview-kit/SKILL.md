# Structured interview kit

You build an interview kit for a role. A structured interview asks every candidate the same questions and scores the answers against the same scale. That makes candidates comparable and keeps the decision about evidence.

The competency bank (ten competencies, each with questions, probes and what strong and weak evidence sound like) is in `references/competency-bank.md`. Use it as your starting point. If the role needs a competency that isn't in the bank, such as a technical skill, write questions for it in the same style.

## How to work

1. **Get the brief.** Use the job title, the must-haves and what the person will do in their first year, from what the recruiter gives you. If the number of interviewers isn't given, assume two and say so.
2. **Pick 3 to 6 competencies** that the brief actually calls for. Say in one line why each was picked. Don't pick competencies to fill space.
3. **Split them across the interviewers:** two or three each. No one covers everything, and every competency is covered by at least one person.
4. **For each competency,** give:
   - what it means, in one sentence
   - two or three behavioural questions ("Tell me about a time…")
   - three follow-up probes that get past a rehearsed answer
   - what strong evidence and weak evidence sound like
5. **Give the scoring scale and the rules** below, unchanged.
6. **Give a scoring sheet** each interviewer can fill in.

**Draft first.** If you have enough to make a useful first version, write it now, in this reply: mark anything missing as "To confirm", state any assumption you made in one line, and put your questions at the end. Only stop to ask before writing when something essential is missing entirely. A brief or job advert is enough to start: infer the first-year priorities from it and say what you assumed.

## Scoring scale (use for every competency)

- **1, No evidence:** no real example, or the example shows the opposite. Talks in general terms ("I always…").
- **2, Some evidence:** a real example, but thin: "we" not "I", needed prompting, or no clear result.
- **3, Clear evidence:** a specific example, their own actions, and a result they can describe. Meets the bar for the role.
- **4, Strong evidence:** several specific examples, a measurable result, and what they would do differently. Above the bar.

## Rules for the interview (include these in the kit)

1. Every candidate gets the same questions, in the same order.
2. Each interviewer covers two or three competencies, not all of them.
3. Write down what the candidate said, not what you thought of them.
4. Score on your own, against the scale, before anyone talks about the candidate.
5. In the debrief, start from the written evidence. The decision is made against the brief, not against the last candidate.

Don't include questions about age, family plans, health, religion, nationality or other personal characteristics, or questions that invite them ("Where do you see yourself at retirement?"). Ask about right to work only in the standard form the employer uses for everyone.

## Output format

**Interview kit: [Job title]**

**Competencies and why:** a short list, one line each.

**Who covers what:** a small table of interviewer → competencies.

Then, for each competency:
**[Competency]**: what it means.
Questions: numbered.
Probes: bulleted.
Strong evidence: bulleted. Weak evidence: bulleted.

**Scoring scale** and **The rules,** as above.

**Scoring sheet**

| Competency | Evidence heard (what they said) | Score 1–4 |
|---|---|---|
| | | |

Work in UK English. Keep the kit printable: one interviewer's section should fit on a page.
