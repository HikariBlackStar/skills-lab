# Competency bank

Ten competencies, each with what it means, behavioural questions, follow-up probes, and what strong and weak evidence sound like. Written by Hikari for the free structured interview kit.

## Ownership

Takes responsibility for an outcome, beyond their own task, and sees it through.

**Questions**
- Tell me about a time you took on a problem that wasn’t strictly yours to fix.
- Tell me about something you delivered that went wrong. What did you do?
- Tell me about a time you noticed something was being dropped between teams.

**Probes**
- What exactly did you do, rather than the team?
- Who else could have picked it up, and why didn’t they?
- What happened in the end, and how do you know?

**Strong evidence sounds like**
- Says "I" for their own actions and "we" for the team’s
- Owns the failure without blaming others
- Follows through after the obvious finish line

**Weak evidence sounds like**
- Waited to be asked
- The story ends with handing it to someone else
- Can’t say what the result was

## Problem solving

Breaks a messy problem down, uses evidence, and gets to a sound decision.

**Questions**
- Tell me about the hardest problem you solved in the last year.
- Tell me about a decision you made with incomplete information.
- Tell me about a time the obvious answer turned out to be wrong.

**Probes**
- What options did you consider and reject?
- What information did you go and get?
- What would you do differently now?

**Strong evidence sounds like**
- Explains how they framed the problem before solving it
- Used data or tested an assumption
- Can name the trade-off they accepted

**Weak evidence sounds like**
- Jumps straight to the answer
- No options considered
- Credits luck or someone else for the insight

## Working with others

Builds trust, shares credit, and works through disagreement.

**Questions**
- Tell me about a time you disagreed with a colleague. How was it resolved?
- Tell me about a time you needed help from someone who had no reason to give it.
- Tell me about a team you made better.

**Probes**
- What was their point of view?
- What did you change your mind about?
- How is the relationship now?

**Strong evidence sounds like**
- Describes the other side fairly
- Changed their own approach
- Relationship intact or better afterwards

**Weak evidence sounds like**
- The other person is the villain
- Won the argument, lost the relationship
- Can’t describe anyone else’s contribution

## Communication

Explains clearly to the audience in front of them, in writing and out loud.

**Questions**
- Tell me about a time you had to explain something complicated to someone outside your field.
- Tell me about a piece of bad news you had to deliver.
- Tell me about something you wrote that changed a decision.

**Probes**
- How did you know they understood?
- What did you leave out, and why?
- What did they do next?

**Strong evidence sounds like**
- Adapted to the listener
- Checked understanding
- Delivered bad news early and plainly

**Weak evidence sounds like**
- Same message for every audience
- Avoided or delayed the hard message
- The answer itself is hard to follow

## Dealing with ambiguity

Makes progress when the goal, the process or the facts are unclear.

**Questions**
- Tell me about a time you started something with no clear brief.
- Tell me about a time priorities changed halfway through your work.
- Tell me about a time you had to decide without the person who normally decides.

**Probes**
- What did you do first?
- What assumptions did you make, and did you check them?
- Who did you keep informed?

**Strong evidence sounds like**
- Created structure: a first step, a plan, a check-in
- Made assumptions explicit
- Kept people informed as things moved

**Weak evidence sounds like**
- Waited for clarity
- Pressed on without checking assumptions
- Frustration is the main memory

## Client and customer focus

Understands what the client actually needs and acts on it.

**Questions**
- Tell me about a time you found out a client wanted something different from what they asked for.
- Tell me about a difficult client and how you handled them.
- Tell me about a time you said no to a client.

**Probes**
- How did you find out what they really needed?
- What did it cost you or the business?
- Are they still a client?

**Strong evidence sounds like**
- Asked questions before acting
- Said no with a reason and an alternative
- Can point to a lasting result for the client

**Weak evidence sounds like**
- Did whatever was asked
- Blames the client
- Measures success by the client being quiet

## Delivering under pressure

Keeps quality and commitments when time or resources are short.

**Questions**
- Tell me about the tightest deadline you’ve hit. What did you give up to hit it?
- Tell me about a time you missed a deadline.
- Tell me about a time you had more work than time.

**Probes**
- How did you decide what came first?
- Who did you tell, and when?
- What did you change afterwards so it didn’t happen again?

**Strong evidence sounds like**
- Prioritised openly and told people early
- Protected what mattered most
- Learnt something and changed a habit

**Weak evidence sounds like**
- Heroics with no plan
- Missed it and nobody knew until late
- Quality dropped and they didn’t notice

## Influence and stakeholders

Gets things done through people they don’t manage.

**Questions**
- Tell me about a time you got a senior person to change their mind.
- Tell me about a project that needed several teams to agree.
- Tell me about a time you couldn’t get the support you needed.

**Probes**
- What did they care about?
- Who did you talk to first, and why?
- What would you do differently?

**Strong evidence sounds like**
- Started from the other person’s interests
- Built support before the big meeting
- Knows when to escalate and how

**Weak evidence sounds like**
- Relied on authority or persistence alone
- Surprised people in the meeting
- No reflection on why it failed

## Commercial awareness

Understands how the work makes or saves money, and acts on it.

**Questions**
- Tell me about a time you saved money or made money for your employer.
- Tell me about a decision where cost and quality pulled in different directions.
- Tell me about how your last team’s results were measured.

**Probes**
- What were the numbers?
- Who else would have weighed it differently?
- How did it show up in the results?

**Strong evidence sounds like**
- Knows the numbers without being asked
- Weighs cost against value, not cost alone
- Links their work to the business result

**Weak evidence sounds like**
- Vague on numbers
- Treats budget as someone else’s problem
- Can’t say how success was measured

## Learning and growth

Learns quickly, seeks feedback, and changes because of it.

**Questions**
- Tell me about the most useful feedback you’ve had. What did you change?
- Tell me about something you had to learn fast for a job.
- Tell me about a mistake you won’t make again.

**Probes**
- How did you go about learning it?
- How did you know you’d got it?
- Who else did you share it with?

**Strong evidence sounds like**
- Asked for feedback rather than waiting for it
- A specific behaviour changed
- Passed the lesson on

**Weak evidence sounds like**
- The feedback was unfair
- Learnt "to work harder"
- Example is years old
