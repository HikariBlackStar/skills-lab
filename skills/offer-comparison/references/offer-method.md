# Offer method and UK rates (2026/27)

Source: Hikari's free offer comparison calculator (hikari-ai.co.uk/tools/offer-comparison-calculator), which takes its figures from GOV.UK, "Rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027" (checked 21 October 2026). Use these figures exactly. Don't add others.

## UK rates

- **Personal allowance:** £12,570, tapered away above £100,000 (it falls by £1 for every £2 of income over £100,000).
- Taxable income = income less the personal allowance. The bands are on TAXABLE income.
- **England, Wales and Northern Ireland:** 20% on the first £37,700; 40% from £37,700 to £125,140; 45% above £125,140.
- **Scotland (six rates):** 19% on the first £3,967; 20% to £16,956; 21% to £31,092; 42% to £62,430; 45% to £125,140; 48% above.
- **Employee National Insurance:** 8% on pay between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% above £50,270.

**Assumptions to state:** standard tax code; pension is a net pay arrangement (the candidate's contribution comes off before income tax but not before NI; salary sacrifice would also save NI, so take-home there would be a little higher); no student loans, benefits-in-kind tax or share schemes.

## The sums, for each offer

1. Gross = salary + bonus.
2. Candidate pension = salary × their %. Employer pension = salary × employer %. (Pension is on salary only, not bonus.)
3. Income tax on (gross − candidate pension), using the bands above.
4. NI on gross.
5. **Take-home a year** = gross − candidate pension − income tax − NI. A month = ÷ 12.
6. Working days = 260 − holiday days.
7. Office days a year = working days × (office days a week ÷ 5).
8. **Commute cost a year** = office days a year × commute cost a day. **Commute hours a year** = office days a year × commute minutes a day ÷ 60.
9. **Real value** = take-home − commute cost + employer pension + candidate pension + other benefits.
10. **Pay per hour, travel included** = (take-home − commute cost) ÷ (working days × 7.5 hours + commute hours a year).

Differences are offer minus baseline. Pension difference compares both contributions added together.
